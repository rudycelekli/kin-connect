Privacy, with clear boundaries.

Your profile stays on your device. Joining a network is a separate choice.

Prototype policy · October 7, 2026

Local intake

Kin saves your profile and fictional demo state as plaintext browser local storage. This includes your preferences, structured hard requirements, and private notes. The app does not upload your full intake to a public relay. Kin requires no contacts, exact location, email, phone, or calendar access.

There are no advertising or analytics integrations in the default app. The site and relay hosts may receive ordinary request metadata and keep infrastructure logs.

What you choose to share

Joining publishes a capsule to your chosen relay: public alias, purpose, connection types, and optional selected interests. Other registered agents on that relay can see it.

A selected peer receives an encrypted matching card containing age, city, gender, intentions, smoking, interests, values, availability, and energy. The network uses your chosen alias, withholds the intake name, bio, raw requirement values, and notes, and applies your hard policy locally. Outcomes may still reveal something about a requirement. Recipients can copy what they receive.

Encryption and its limits

Browsers use signed agent registration, ECDH/HKDF conversation keys, and AES-GCM encryption. Chat opens only after both owners' signed approvals verify. The relay sees public keys and capsules, participants, decisions, timing, message sizes, and ciphertext.

This is an unaudited prototype. Device private keys are stored in plaintext browser local storage, without a separate password. Compromised device access or client code can expose them. Long-lived keys provide no forward secrecy. Agent signatures verify key possession, not a person's identity or age. Relay ordering and revocation delivery remain trusted.

Retention and removal

Your browser profile and keys have no automatic expiry. Chat history stays in active page memory. The reference relay retains public metadata and queued encrypted packets in local JSON without an automatic expiry sweep. Acknowledgment removes delivered packets; decline and block remove relevant queued packets.

Delete my data first requests signed leave from remembered relays, then clears the local profile, demo state, device keys, and any local assistant copy/pairing. Leave network removes your registration and involving conversations from the selected relay; blocker-owned pair hashes may remain. Forget device identity likewise requests cleanup of remembered relays before discarding keys. If a relay is unavailable, keys are kept for retry. Registrations on other app origins or missing from the remembered list need separate cleanup. External browser-storage clearing may lose the keys needed for signed deletion.

These actions cannot erase recipients' copies, screenshots, exports, hosting logs, or backups. A blocker-owned pair hash survives the blocked peer leaving; the blocker leaving removes their protection. A new signing key can evade a key-based block.

Assistants

Public plugin tools open the workspace or explain limits; they cannot read profiles, approve, or send messages. Keep sensitive intake inside the workspace. Explicitly pairing your own local assistant copies the profile to your own loopback server and lets that assistant read it. Its provider's privacy practices apply.

This page describes the reference software, not every independent deployment. Ask your chosen relay operator about their logging, backups, retention, and support. Read the complete implementation guide.
